## 1. What DCA Actually Solves

The definition is simple: **a fixed interval, a fixed amount, buying the same asset**. For example, buy 200 USDT of Bitcoin on the 1st of every month, and repeat.

It addresses three problems:

| Problem | How DCA handles it |
|:---|:---|
| You don't know when to buy | Time diversification replaces price prediction |
| Emotions drive decisions | The rules are set in advance — you buy on red days too |
| Single-entry risk | Capital enters in tranches instead of one large position |

Be clear about what it does **not** solve: if the asset trends down for a long stretch, DCA still loses money — just more slowly than a lump sum. DCA reduces *timing risk*, not *asset risk*.

## 3. Who DCA Suits — and Who It Does Not

**Relatively suited:**&#8203;

- Long-term conviction, but you admit you cannot time the market
- Stable cash flow (salary, rent) where each contribution does not affect your life
- You don't want to watch charts daily and prefer automating the decision

**Relatively unsuited:**&#8203;

- You need the money within six months
- You expect DCA to double your money quickly
- You cannot handle a -50% paper drawdown
- You want to "DCA" with leverage or futures (a completely different risk level)

## 4. Lump Sum vs. Recurring Buys: Six Comparisons

| Dimension | Lump Sum | Recurring Buys (DCA) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Timing requirement | High — entry price drives most of the outcome | Low — time does the work |
| Emotional pressure | High — easy to chase or panic sell | Low — rule-driven |
| Capital efficiency | High — money works immediately | Low — capital enters gradually |
| Sustained uptrend | Usually better than DCA | Usually lags |
| Sustained downtrend | Usually worse than DCA | Usually better |
| Effort | One action | Ongoing, needs maintenance |

Being honest: **in a sustained uptrend, a lump sum usually beats DCA. In a sustained downtrend, DCA usually beats a lump sum.** DCA is not "the better method" — it is "the method more people can actually finish." Most failures come from never completing the plan, not from picking the wrong one.

| Parameter | Description | Typical choice |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Asset | What you are buying | Spot pairs such as BTC/USDT |
| Amount per period | How much quote currency each time | Based on cash flow — start small |
| Frequency | How often it executes | Daily / weekly / biweekly / monthly |
| Execution and stop | When to start, when to end | Scheduled time, total periods, or until you stop it |

**Choosing a frequency:**&#8203;

- **Daily**: closest to true average cost, most orders — heavier load on fees and minimum order sizes
- **Weekly / biweekly**: a middle ground most people settle on
- **Monthly**: fewest orders, aligns with pay cycles, but coarser price sampling

Frequency does not change the nature of long-term average cost. It only changes sampling granularity and the number of fee events.

## 8. A Six-Period Worked Example (Illustrative)

Assume 100 USDT per period for 6 periods (**all numbers below are illustrative and exclude fees and spread**):

| Period | Price (USDT) | BTC bought |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | 60,000 | 0.00166667 |
| 2 | 55,000 | 0.00181818 |
| 3 | 50,000 | 0.00200000 |
| 4 | 48,000 | 0.00208333 |
| 5 | 52,000 | 0.00192308 |
| 6 | 58,000 | 0.00172414 |
| **Total** | — | **~0.0112154 BTC** |

- Total invested: 600 USDT, total BTC acquired: ~0.0112154
- Average cost = 600 ÷ 0.0112154 ≈ **53,497 USDT**
- The arithmetic average of the six prices = 53,833 USDT

Notice that **the DCA average cost (53,497) is lower than the simple average price (53,833)**&#8203; — that is the core effect of averaging: cheaper periods buy more coins, pulling your cost down.

Comparing two approaches:

| Approach | Result at a final price of 58,000 (illustrative) |
|:---|:---|
| DCA over 6 periods | Position value ≈ 650.49 USDT, about +8.4% |
| Lump sum of 600 USDT in period 1 | Position value 580 USDT, about -3.3% |

**But that is only the result for this specific path.** If price had run from 60,000 straight to 90,000, the lump sum would have won decisively. The example exists to show you the mechanism, not to claim DCA is always better.

## 10. Three Disciplines and Five Common Mistakes

**Three disciplines:**&#8203;

1. **Size it so a drawdown never affects your life** — DCA's enemy is forced selling, not volatility
2. **Do not stop during dips** — stopping usually erases the averaging effect
3. **Do not constantly change amount or frequency** — unless your cash flow actually changed

**Five common mistakes:**&#8203;

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|:---|:---|
| Treating DCA as capital protection | DCA does not protect principal; sustained downtrends still lose |
| Stopping on dips, adding on pumps | That is reverse DCA — you end up buying the expensive zone |
| Setting the amount too large | If cash flow breaks, you are forced to sell low |
| Ignoring fees and spread | With small, frequent orders, cost drag becomes material |
| Concentrating everything on one custodian | Exchange assets carry platform risk — consider sizing and self-custody |
