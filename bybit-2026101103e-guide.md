TradFi (traditional finance) covers forex, gold and commodities, and global equity indices. Bybit packages these markets so they can be traded with crypto assets (such as USDT) as margin — letting you access them **without opening a separate traditional brokerage account**.

| Item | Bybit TradFi (traditional markets) | Crypto futures |
|:---|:---|:---|

## 4. Step 2: Read the Order Interface (Field by Field)

| Field | What to choose | Common beginner mistake |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Instrument | The asset (gold, forex pair, index) | Not noticing which market or session it belongs to |
| Direction | Long (bullish) / short (bearish) | Confusing direction with "buy" |
| Lots / notional | Position size | Guessing instead of sizing by risk |
| Leverage | The multiplier | Starting at the maximum |
| Required margin | Shown by the system | Not checking the balance, causing a rejected order |
| Stop-loss / take-profit | Exit levels set in advance | Placing first and thinking about it later |
| Order type | Market / limit / stop | Using market orders in illiquid hours and eating slippage |

**Recommended order:**&#8203; pick the instrument → decide direction → size the position and leverage → set stop-loss and take-profit → then submit.

## 5. Step 3: Choose the Right Order Type

| Order type | When to use | Watch out for |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Market | You want immediate execution | Wider slippage in illiquid hours |
| Limit | You want a specific price | May not fill; requires patience |
| Stop-loss | Cap losses in advance | Extreme moves may not fill at your price |
| Take-profit | Lock in profit in advance | Same gap and slippage caveats |

> Beginner tip: **enter with limit orders and protect with stop-losses**; avoid chasing price with market orders in quiet hours.

## 6. Step 4: Place Your First Order

1. Open the TradFi order screen and confirm sufficient balance.
2. Choose an instrument you understand (beginners should start with the most liquid majors).
3. Choose direction and leverage (**start with the lowest leverage to learn the flow**).
4. Enter the position size and check the required margin shown.
5. Set stop-loss and take-profit levels.
6. Choose the order type (a limit order is recommended), verify price and quantity, then submit.
7. Confirm the filled order in your positions list, and note your entry reason and exit plan.

> The goal of your first order is not profit — it is **completing the flow and understanding the interface**. Keep the size as small as possible.

## 7. Step 5: Monitor and Exit

- **Watch P&L, not emotions.** Set stop-losses and take-profits, then stop moving them around.
- **Trailing stop.** As price moves in your favor, raise the stop gradually to reduce risk.
- **Mind overnight costs.** Holding past the daily cut-off may incur financing/rollover fees that **accumulate over time**.
- **Decide before the weekend.** If you do not want weekend gap risk, close positions before the market closes.
- **Do not average down into a losing position.** It is one of the most common causes of blown accounts.
