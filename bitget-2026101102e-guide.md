## 6. Three Things to Watch When Buying Small Amounts

1. **Minimum order size.** Every market has a minimum; amounts below it cannot be submitted (**see the app for specifics**).
2. **Fee as a share of cost.** Buying $100 versus $100,000 means a very different **absolute** fee but the same **percentage**; at small sizes, watch whether spreads and fixed costs eat the edge.
3. **Spread and slippage.** A market order in thin hours can fill well away from the price you saw.

> This is exactly why **limit orders plus a low fee rate** is the beginner combination: you control the price and reduce round-trip cost.

1. **Decide custody.** Keeping it on the exchange is **custodial** (convenient, platform-dependent); moving it to your own wallet is **self-custody** (more control, but a lost seed phrase cannot be recovered). Beginners can start on the exchange and learn self-custody later.
2. **Record your cost basis.** Note price, quantity, and time — you will need this for P&L and tax reporting.
3. **Do not trade frequently.** After buying, the most common source of loss is not picking the wrong asset but **fees and emotional trading**. BTC rewards sitting still for a while.

## 8. Getting Started with DCA

If you do not want to guess entry timing, consider **dollar-cost averaging**:

- **How:**&#8203; invest a fixed amount at fixed intervals (weekly or monthly) into BTC.
- **Benefit:**&#8203; you buy less when prices are high and more when they are low, smoothing your average cost.
- **Limit:**&#8203; **DCA does not guarantee profit.** It only spreads the risk of mis-timing; it cannot prevent losses in a prolonged decline.
- **Key:**&#8203; once you set the schedule, **execute it mechanically** — do not increase the amount after a rally or stop after a dip.
