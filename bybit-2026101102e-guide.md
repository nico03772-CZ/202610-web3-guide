## 1. Perpetual vs Inverse in One Sentence

- **USDT perpetual (linear):**&#8203; priced, margined, and settled in **USDT**. You earn USDT.
- **Inverse contract (coin-margined):**&#8203; priced in **USD**, but margined and settled in the **underlying coin** (e.g. BTC). You earn coin.

In short: **one is USD-denominated, the other is coin-denominated.** That single difference cascades into margin behavior, P&L curves, and risk profile.

## 3. Four Key Differences Explained

### Difference 1: Different pricing and settlement currencies

USDT perpetuals quote and settle in USDT, so your P&L is straightforward. Inverse contracts are priced in USD but settled in coin — **the price you see is in dollars, but the profit you receive is in coin**. For the same USD notional, the actual quantity of coin gained or lost moves with price.

### Difference 2: Different margin currency

USDT perpetuals use USDT margin, which is relatively stable. Inverse contracts use the **underlying coin itself** as margin, so the USD value of your margin moves with price. This makes **margin ratio changes more sensitive**.

### Difference 3: Linear vs non-linear P&L

P&L on USDT perpetuals is roughly proportional to price moves (linear). Because inverse contracts settle in coin, **the same percentage price move produces different USD-equivalent P&L at different price levels** — that is the non-linearity. Practically: the lower the price, the more coin a given notional represents.

### Difference 4: Different audiences

- **USDT perpetual:**&#8203; suits most traders, especially those who want all P&L managed in a stablecoin and do not want to hold the underlying coin.
- **Inverse contract:**&#8203; suits people who **already hold the coin long term** (coin holders, miners). Going long and seeing price rise increases your coin holdings; but if price falls, losses are also in coin while your margin shrinks — a **double squeeze**.
