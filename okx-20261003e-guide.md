## 1. What Is Bitcoin? Three Basics to Get Right First

Bitcoin (ticker: **BTC**) is a **decentralized digital asset** that launched in 2009. It has no headquarters, belongs to no single company or government, and is not issued by a central authority. Instead, a global network of computers maintains a public ledger that anyone can use to send, receive, and verify.

Before you spend any money, lock in these three ideas:

| Basic | What it means | Why it matters |
|:---|:---|:---|
| It is **not a stock** | Holding Bitcoin pays no dividend and gives you no ownership in a company | You cannot judge its price with earnings multiples or financial statements |
| It has **no central issuer** | The supply cap lives in the code; no central bank can print more | Scarcity is the core of its value proposition |
| Its **value comes from market consensus** | Price is set by buyers and sellers matching in real time | Volatility is high, and moves often need no "reason" |

Get these three straight and you are already ahead of half the people who merely repeat what a friend told them.

## 2. Why Bitcoin Has Value: Four Sources

"Why is a string of code worth money?" is the most common question. The answer has four layers.

### 1. Scarcity

Bitcoin has a hard supply cap of **21 million coins**, written into the protocol and not subject to unilateral change. New supply also slows down through a **halving** roughly every four years. Scarcity that is predictable is a big reason it gets called "digital gold."

### 2. A Publicly Verifiable Ledger

Every Bitcoin transfer is recorded on the **blockchain**, and anyone can look up the history of an address. That means "does this money exist" and "was it ever moved" can be independently verified instead of taken on trust from a statement.

### 3. Transferability

Bitcoin transfers do not follow bank opening hours; the network runs **24/7 and across borders**. With an internet connection and the right private key, you can send value to another person on the planet without routing through a traditional bank.

### 4. Market Consensus and Institutional Participation

As more institutions, funds, and individuals treat it as an asset class to allocate to, the consensus gets reinforced. Consensus does not guarantee a higher price, but it explains why the asset keeps existing.

> ⚠️ A reminder: those four points explain why Bitcoin gets *used*. They do **not** mean the price only goes up. Bitcoin has seen multiple drawdowns of more than 50%.
