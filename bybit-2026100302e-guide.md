## 2. The Core Formula: Notional × Rate

This is the one line worth memorizing:

> **Fee = notional value × rate**
> **Notional value = quantity × execution price**

**The critical point: fees are charged on notional, not on your margin.**

## 3. Maker vs Taker: Why the Same Trade Costs Different Amounts

This is one of the biggest sources of fee differences.

| Role | What you do | What you contribute | Rate |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Maker** | Post a limit order and wait to be filled | Provide liquidity | Lower, sometimes better |
| **Taker** | Send an order that fills immediately against resting orders | Consume liquidity | Higher |

**How to tell which you are:**&#8203; if your order fills the instant you send it, you are a Taker. If it sits on the book waiting, you are a Maker.

| Order method | Usual role |
|:---|:---|
| Market order | Taker |
| Quick buy | Usually Taker |
| Limit order (priced away from the market) | Maker |
| Limit order (priced at the market, fills instantly) | May become Taker |

**Practical takeaway:**&#8203; to cut fees, learn to post limit orders. The trade-off is that you may never get filled — you are **exchanging cost for certainty of execution**, and it is not free.

**In one line:**&#8203; spot costs are one-off; futures costs are one-off **plus ongoing**. That is why short-term futures traders are especially rate-sensitive.

## 5. Funding Rates Are Not Trading Fees

This is the most misunderstood item:

| What people say | Reality |
|:---|:---|
| "The funding rate is a fee the platform charges" | No. It is **paid between longs and shorts**; the platform does not take it |
| "It only applies when you open" | No. It **settles periodically** while a position is open (a common design is every 8 hours; confirm via official announcements) |
| "It is always a cost" | No. When the rate is positive, longs pay shorts; when negative, the reverse. **You may be the one receiving it** |

**Why care?**&#8203; Because on a long hold, funding steadily affects your margin, and it is **not covered by the referral discount.** That is the real hidden cost of holding futures.
