## 2. Three Benefits of Following Multiple Traders

1. **Diversifies one trader's luck**
   A single trader's short-term run may come from one well-timed trend. Spreading across several reduces the chance of following someone right before a drawdown.

2. **Different styles can complement each other**
   Some are good at ranging scalps, others at trend holding. Different styles often perform differently in the same market, which can smooth the combined curve.

3. **Avoids single-point risk**
   With one trader, their stop-out, disconnect, or decision to stop leading directly hits your entire position.

## 3. Four Risks You Must Recognize

### Risk 1: Correlation risk — you may not actually be diversified

This is **the most overlooked and the most damaging** point.

If three traders are all long BTC, your "diversification" is really the same risk tripled. When the market turns, all three positions lose **at the same time**.

**How to check:**&#8203; review their current direction and instruments regularly. If they overlap heavily, you are concentrated, not diversified.

### Risk 2: Margin usage quietly raises your effective leverage

Every copy position consumes margin. The more traders you follow, the less free balance remains, **pushing your account's effective leverage higher**. In a sharp move, that makes the account more fragile.

### Risk 3: Fees and costs multiplied by trade count

The more traders you follow, the more trades the system executes on your behalf, and the more fees accumulate. **Copy trading is a high-frequency activity**, which magnifies the value of a fee discount.

### Risk 4: Management complexity jumps

With one trader you can watch them. With five, you only see results. If one drifts in style or starts a losing streak, you may not notice in time.
