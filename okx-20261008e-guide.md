## 1. The Difference in One Line: Custody vs Self-Custody

- **The exchange**: you register an account, your assets sit on the platform's ledger, and the platform holds them for you. Forget your password and you can recover through a verification flow;
- **The Web3 wallet**: you hold a seed phrase and private keys, and the assets live on-chain under your control. **If the seed phrase is lost or obtained by someone else, the assets cannot be recovered or may be taken.**

One is "the platform holds it for you," the other is "you hold it yourself" — every other difference flows from here.

## 3. The Three Most Important Differences

### Difference 1: Asset Recovery Works Completely Differently

An exchange account can be recovered via email and verification codes. A Web3 wallet has no such mechanism — **the seed phrase is the only key and the only backup.** That's the strength of self-custody (nobody can freeze or move your assets) and also its barrier (no support desk can retrieve them for you).

### Difference 2: Different Fee Structures

The exchange charges **trading fees** per trade. A Web3 wallet pays **gas** for on-chain actions, which fluctuates with network congestion, plus slippage and liquidity pool fees on decentralized swaps. These are different cost logics and can't be compared directly.

### Difference 3: Risk Comes From Different Directions

- Exchange risk leans toward **platform and account level**: account theft, phishing sites, leaked verification codes;
- Web3 wallet risk leans toward **operational and on-chain level**: leaked seed phrases, signing malicious approvals, phishing sites, interacting with unknown contracts.

In short: using an exchange means trusting the platform's security systems; using a self-custody wallet means carrying the security responsibility yourself.

## 6. Security Priorities for Each Side

| Exchange account | Web3 wallet |
| :--- | :--- |

In one line: **the exchange outsources security to systems; the wallet puts security in your own hands.** Both require active protection — neither can be fully hands-off.
